User-Centered Design (UCD) 🧑‍💻🔄

Designing with the user at the heart of every decision! 💡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • User-centered design (UCD) – Design process involving users at all stages
  • User – Person using or affected by a product
  • Empathy – Understanding users' needs, values, and beliefs
  • Task – What a product is supposed to do (and sub-uses)
  • Environment – Where and how a product is used
  • Useful – Solves a real problem
  • Usable – Easy and intuitive to use
  • Desirable – People want to use it
  • Iterative – Repeating process to improve design
  • Norman door – Poorly designed door that confuses users

Core Concepts 🧠

What is User-Centered Design?

  • Involves users at all stages of design
  • Focuses on users' needs, tasks, and environments
  • Leads to more intuitive, successful products

User & Empathy

  • User: anyone using or affected by a product
  • Empathy: understanding users' thoughts, needs, and feelings
  • Key to great design ideas

Tasks & Environment

  • Task: what the product is supposed to do (and sub-uses)
  • Environment: where/how it's used (e.g., car, kitchen)
  • Design should fit both

Useful, Usable, Desirable

  • All three are needed for a successful product
  • Like a stool with three legs—remove one, it falls!

UCD vs. Conventional Design

  • UCD: user is the center, involved throughout
  • Conventional: designer/manufacturer decides, then pushes to market
  • UCD leads to better, more innovative products

Iterative Process

  • Design is improved through repeated cycles
  • User feedback is used to refine the product
  • Continues even after launch

The UCD Cycle

  • Research: context studies, focus groups, interviews, personas
  • Concept: models, scenarios, prototypes, usability testing
  • Design: structure, wireframes, interactive prototypes, accessibility
  • Implementation: testing with end users, expert evaluation
  • Launch: product released, feedback continues the cycle

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Norman doors are everywhere—look for confusing push/pull doors!
  • Apple's simple remote is a great example of UCD; most people only use a few buttons.
  • UCD can lead to unexpected innovations from real user feedback.
  • UCD is a cycle—products keep improving after launch!

Quick Quiz ⚡

What are the three legs of a successful product in UCD?
Model Answer: Useful, usable, and desirable.
What is a Norman door?
Model Answer: A poorly designed door that confuses users about whether to push or pull.
Why is UCD considered iterative?
Model Answer: Because it repeats cycles of user feedback and improvement, even after launch.
