Waste Mitigation Strategies ♻️🗑️

Why does it matter? Waste mitigation is about using less stuff and less energy—helping the planet and saving resources for the future! 🌍

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Dematerialization 📉 – Using less material and energy in products and processes
  • Embodied energy ⚡ – Total energy used to make, transport, use, and dispose of a product
  • Reuse 🔁 – Using a product again for the same or a new purpose
  • Repair 🛠️ – Fixing a product to extend its life
  • Recycle ♻️ – Turning waste into new products
  • Recondition 🔧 – Restoring a product to like-new condition
  • Re-engineer 💡 – Redesigning to improve efficiency or reduce material/energy use

What is Dematerialization? 📉

Less Stuff, Less Energy

Dematerialization means using fewer resources and less energy. It's about making products lighter, more efficient, and less wasteful—good for the planet and your wallet!

Embodied Energy Example 🥫⚡

An aluminum can has about 4 megajoules of embodied energy! That's the total energy from mining bauxite, smelting, manufacturing, transporting, filling, and even recycling or dumping the can.

Fun Fact: A liter of petrol contains about 34.2 megajoules of energy—almost 9 times more than a can!

The 5 Rs of Waste Mitigation 🔁🛠️♻️🔧💡

Reuse 🔁

Use a product again, either for the same purpose or a new one.

Example: Making a vertical garden from old plastic bottles.

Repair 🛠️

Fixing broken products instead of throwing them away.

Example: The TV show The Repair Shop—restoring clocks, ceramics, and more!

Recycle ♻️

Turning waste materials into new products.

Example: Plastic bricks made from recycled bottles, used to build schools in the Ivory Coast.

Recondition 🔧

Restoring used products to like-new condition.

Example: Retreading old tires to give them new life.

Re-engineer 💡

Redesigning products to use less material or energy, or to last longer.

Example: The evolution of light bulbs: from energy-hungry incandescents to efficient LEDs.
