Strategies for User-Centered Design 🧑‍🔬🛠️

How do you gather the best data for great design? 🔍

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Field research – Gathering data in the user's natural environment
  • Method of extremes – Designing for the 5th to 95th percentile of users
  • Observation – Watching users interact with a product
  • Interview – One-on-one discussion to gather user feedback
  • Focus group – Group interview to collect diverse opinions
  • Questionnaire – Survey to collect data from many users
  • Affinity diagramming – Grouping ideas/facts into themes
  • Participatory design – Actively involving users in the design process
  • Prototype – Early model of a product for testing
  • Usability testing – Testing how easy and effective a product is to use
  • Natural environment – Real-world setting where a product is used
  • Usability laboratory – Controlled environment for testing products

Core Concepts 🧠

Field Research

  • Gathering data in the user's real environment
  • Reduces artificial effects of lab testing
  • Can reveal unrecognized issues

Method of Extremes

  • Design for the 5th to 95th percentile
  • Accommodates most users
  • Examples: doorways, furniture for children

Observation

  • Watch users interact with a product
  • Can be in the field or in a lab
  • Helps spot usability issues

Interviews & Focus Groups

  • Interviews: one-on-one, in-depth feedback
  • Focus groups: group discussion, dynamic, observe body language
  • Clarifying questions possible

Questionnaires

  • Surveys for large sample sizes
  • Cheap, easy to distribute
  • Responses may be biased to extremes

Affinity Diagramming

  • Group facts, opinions, or ideas into themes
  • Helps visualize and organize data
  • Builds teamwork

Participatory Design

  • Actively involve users in the design process
  • Can use paper prototypes or real products
  • Users help shape the design

Prototypes & Usability Testing

  • Prototypes: early models for testing
  • Usability testing: see how easy/effective a product is
  • Can be done in natural or lab environments

Natural vs. Lab Environments

  • Natural: real-world, authentic, mostly qualitative data
  • Lab: controlled, can use sensors, more observers, quantitative data

Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Field: authentic, detailed, but narrow and time-consuming
  • Lab: controlled, measurable, but costly and less natural

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Usability labs often have one-way mirrors so observers can watch without being seen!
  • Affinity diagramming is used by top companies to organize ideas after brainstorming.
  • Participatory design can include users as "co-designers" of products.
