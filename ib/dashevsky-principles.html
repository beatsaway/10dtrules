Dashevsky's Five Principles of Sustainable Design 🔄☀️🛡️⚡🤝

Five keys to designing for a better world! 🌍

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Cyclic – Use recycled/compostable materials, close the loop
  • Solar – Use renewable energy for manufacturing and use
  • Safe – Non-toxic, non-polluting byproducts
  • Efficient – Less energy, materials, and water than older products
  • Social – Uphold human rights, fair trade, safe work, and justice
  • Triple bottom line – People, planet, profit (social, environmental, economic)

Core Concepts 🧠

Cyclic

  • Use recycled or compostable materials
  • Design for closed-loop systems (cradle to cradle)
  • Example: bioplastics, recycled steel

Solar

  • Use renewable energy (solar, wind, hydro, etc.)
  • Reduce reliance on fossil fuels
  • Example: solar-powered devices, wind-powered factories

Safe

  • Non-toxic, non-polluting byproducts
  • Protect air, water, land, and people
  • Example: hydrogen fuel cell cars (water as byproduct)

Efficient

  • Use less energy, materials, and water
  • Lower costs and environmental impact
  • Example: LED bulbs, efficient appliances

Social

  • Support human rights, fair trade, safe work, and justice
  • Example: fair trade products, safe working conditions

Triple Bottom Line Connections

  • Cyclic, solar, safe, and efficient = environmental & economic
  • Social = social sustainability
  • All five together = true sustainable design

Real-World Examples

  • Bioplastics (cyclic), solar panels (solar), non-toxic paints (safe), LED bulbs (efficient), fair trade chocolate (social)

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Some companies design products to be taken apart with just a screwdriver—easy to recycle!
  • Solar-powered calculators have been around since the 1970s.
  • Fair trade certification helps ensure better wages and working conditions for farmers.
