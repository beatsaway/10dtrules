Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Smart materials – Materials that change properties in response to external stimuli
  • Piezoelectricity – Generates electric charge when deformed
  • Shape memory alloy (Nitinol) – Remembers its shape and returns to it when heated
  • Photochromicity – Changes transparency/shade in response to light
  • Electro/magnetorheostatic – Changes viscosity in response to electric/magnetic fields
  • Thermoelectricity – Produces electricity from heat differences
  • Elasticity – Returns to original shape after deformation
  • Plasticity – Can be permanently deformed

Core Concepts 🧠

Smart Materials

Materials that change their properties in response to external stimuli (like heat, light, pressure, or electricity). Used in everything from medical devices to sunglasses!

Piezoelectricity

Generates a small electric charge when deformed. Example: Piezo lighters, microphones.

Shape Memory Alloy (Nitinol)

Remembers its original shape and returns to it when heated. Example: Medical stents, eyeglass frames.

Photochromicity

Changes transparency or shade in response to light. Example: Photochromic sunglasses that darken in sunlight.

Electro/Magnetorheostatic

Changes viscosity (how easily it flows) in response to electric or magnetic fields. Example: Smart fluids in car suspensions.

Thermoelectricity

Produces electricity from heat differences. Example: Flashlights powered by the heat of your hand.

Elasticity vs Plasticity

Elasticity: Returns to original shape after being deformed (e.g., rubber band).
Plasticity: Can be permanently deformed (e.g., bent paperclip).

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Nitinol is used in medical stents and eyeglass frames for its shape memory!
  • Piezoelectric materials are in lighters and microphones.
  • Thermoelectric flashlights can be powered by your hand's heat.
