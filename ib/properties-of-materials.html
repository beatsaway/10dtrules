Properties of Materials 🔬🧱

From bridges to bling—material properties matter! ✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Density – Mass per unit volume (g/cm³ or kg/m³)
  • Electrical resistivity – How well a material resists electrical flow
  • Thermal conductivity – How well a material conducts heat
  • Thermal expansion – How much a material expands when heated
  • Hardness – Resistance to scratching or penetration
  • Tensile strength – Resistance to pulling forces
  • Compression – Resistance to being squashed
  • Stiffness – Resistance to bending or deflection
  • Toughness – Resistance to crack propagation
  • Ductility – Ability to be drawn into wire or extended shape
  • Aesthetics – How a material looks, feels, smells, or tastes

Core Concepts 🧠

Physical Properties

  • Density: Mass per unit volume. Important for weight, packaging, and design.
  • Electrical resistivity: How well a material resists electrical flow. Low = good conductor (e.g., copper), high = good insulator (e.g., rubber).
  • Thermal conductivity: How well a material conducts heat. Metals = high, wood = low.
  • Thermal expansion: How much a material expands when heated. Bridges and sidewalks have expansion joints for this!
  • Hardness: Resistance to scratching or penetration. Diamond is hardest, talc is softest (Mohs scale).

Mechanical Properties

  • Tensile strength: Resistance to pulling forces (e.g., bridge cables).
  • Compression: Resistance to being squashed (e.g., bricks in a wall).
  • Stiffness: Resistance to bending or deflection.
  • Toughness: Resistance to crack propagation. Hard ≠ tough! Glass is hard but not tough.
  • Ductility: Ability to be drawn into wire or extended shape (e.g., aluminum wire).

Aesthetic Characteristics

How a material looks, feels, smells, or tastes. Aesthetics are subjective and influenced by culture, fashion, and personal taste.
Example: Ornate vs. minimalist furniture.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Instant noodles are sold by weight and volume—density matters!
  • Bridges and sidewalks have expansion joints to prevent buckling from thermal expansion.
  • Diamonds are hard but not tough—don't hit them with a hammer!

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between hardness and toughness?
Model Answer: Hardness is resistance to scratching; toughness is resistance to crack propagation (e.g., glass is hard but not tough).
Why do bridges have expansion joints?
Model Answer: To allow for thermal expansion and contraction, preventing buckling or cracking.
What is ductility?
Model Answer: The ability of a material to be drawn into a wire or extended shape (e.g., aluminum wire).
