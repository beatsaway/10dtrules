Resources & Reserves 🌍⛏️

Why does it matter? Understanding resources and reserves helps designers make sustainable choices and plan for the future. The planet's resources aren't infinite—let's design smart! ♻️

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Resource 🌳 – Any natural material that can be used by humans (e.g., water, wood, oil, minerals).
  • Reserve 🛢️ – The known amount of a resource that can be economically and legally extracted with current technology.
  • Renewable Resource ☀️ – Can be replaced naturally in a short time (e.g., solar, wind, timber).
  • Non-renewable Resource 🦖 – Cannot be replaced quickly (e.g., coal, oil, metals).

Types of Resources 🗂️

  • Natural resources: Found in nature (e.g., water, minerals, forests)
  • Human resources: People and their skills
  • Physical resources: Man-made items (e.g., machines, buildings)
  • Energy resources: Sources of power (e.g., fossil fuels, wind, solar)
Fun Fact: The world's largest gold reserve is in the United States—at Fort Knox! 🏦
Fun Fact: The rare metal neodymium is essential for high-tech electronics and green energy devices. Most of the world's supply is found in China! 🪙🇨🇳

Resources vs. Reserves 🤔

What's the difference?

Resource: The total amount of a material in the ground (even if we can't get it all out yet).
Reserve: The part we can actually extract and use right now, with current technology.

Example: There might be a lot of gold deep underground, but only some is a reserve because it's too expensive or hard to reach the rest.

Real-World Relevance 🌐

  • Designers must consider if materials are sustainable and available for the long term
  • Using renewable resources helps protect the planet
  • Non-renewable resources are running out—think before you design!
  • Recycling and reusing materials can extend resource life
Design Tip: Choose materials that are abundant, renewable, or recyclable whenever possible! ♻️

Sample Questions 📝

Q1: What is the difference between a resource and a reserve?
Model Answer: A resource is the total amount of a material in the ground, while a reserve is the part we can extract and use with current technology and at current prices.
Q2: Give two examples of renewable and non-renewable resources.
Model Answer: Renewable: solar energy, timber. Non-renewable: oil, coal.
