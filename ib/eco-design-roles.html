Eco Design: Roles & Responsibilities 🌿🤝

Why does it matter? Everyone—designers, manufacturers, and consumers—plays a part in making products greener and more sustainable! 🌍✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Disassembly 🛠️ – Designing products so they're easy to take apart for repair or recycling
  • Quality control ✅ – Making sure products are made well, with less waste
  • Market pull 🛒 – When consumer demand drives greener products
  • Triple bottom line 🔺 – People, planet, and profit—all must benefit for true sustainability
  • Converging technologies 📱 – Combining different techs into one device (like a smartphone!)

Roles in Eco Design 🧑‍🎨🏭🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Designer

Designers control the early stages: choose fewer materials, design for energy efficiency, make products easy to disassemble, minimize parts, and use recyclable materials.
Example: A phone designed with a removable battery and minimal screws.

Manufacturer

Manufacturers control most of the product's life cycle: use efficient techniques, ensure quality, standardize parts, minimize emissions, and follow LCA principles.
Example: Using injection molding to reduce plastic waste.

Consumer

Consumers choose what to buy, how to use and maintain it, and how to dispose of it. Their choices drive market pull!
Example: Choosing a reusable water bottle and recycling it at end of life.

UNEP & Triple Bottom Line 🔺🌍

People

Social equity, good working conditions, literacy, health, women's status, and more.
Example: Fair trade chocolate supports farmers and communities.

Planet

Protecting ecosystems, using renewables, reducing toxics, treating emissions, stopping deforestation, and more.
Example: Using solar panels and recycling water.

Profit

Value for customers and stakeholders, fair business, fair prices, and credit for entrepreneurs.
Example: Microloans help small businesses grow sustainably.

Converging Technologies 📱🤖

What is it?

Combining different technologies into one device—like a smartphone (phone, camera, music player, computer, etc.).

Advantages

  • One device, many functions
  • Reduces need for multiple devices
  • Miniaturizes and increases portability
  • Reduces material and energy use

Disadvantages

  • If one part fails, others may too
  • Some functions may not work as well as dedicated devices
  • Repairs can be more complex

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • Microloans have helped millions of entrepreneurs in developing countries start green businesses!
  • Some smartphones now use recycled ocean plastic in their cases.
  • Fair trade chocolate helps ensure better pay and working conditions for farmers.
