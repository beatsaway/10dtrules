Rapid Prototyping ⚡🖨️

From idea to object—rapid prototyping makes it real, fast! 🚀

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Rapid prototyping – Quickly producing a complete product (with internal detail) using digital tools
  • Additive manufacturing – Building up a product by adding material (e.g., 3D printing)
  • Subtractive manufacturing – Creating a product by removing material (e.g., milling)
  • 3D printing – Additive process that builds objects layer by layer
  • Milling – Subtractive process that removes material using a rotating tool
  • CNC – Computer Numerical Control; machines controlled by computers (e.g., CNC router)
  • Axis (axes) – Directions of movement (X, Y, Z); 3-axis or 5-axis machines
  • Filament – Material (often plastic) used in 3D printing
  • Resin – Liquid material hardened by light in some 3D printers

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Rapid Prototyping?

Rapid prototyping is the fast production of a complete product (with internal detail) using digital tools. 3D printing is the most famous example!

Additive vs Subtractive Manufacturing

Additive: Adds material layer by layer (e.g., 3D printing).
Subtractive: Removes material from a block (e.g., milling, CNC).

3D Printing

Builds objects by adding melted or fused material layer by layer. Used for everything from prototypes to fashion to houses!

Milling, CNC, and Axes

Milling: Subtractive process using a rotating tool.
CNC: Computer-controlled machines (e.g., CNC router).
Axes: 3-axis (X, Y, Z) or 5-axis (adds rotation for more complex shapes).

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • 3D printing is now used for everything from fashion to houses!
  • Our school's CNC router is 3-axis, but industry uses 5-axis for complex parts.

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between additive and subtractive manufacturing?
Model Answer: Additive builds up material (e.g., 3D printing); subtractive removes material (e.g., milling).
What does a CNC router do?
Model Answer: It uses computer control to cut or shape material, often in 3 axes (X, Y, Z).
What is filament in 3D printing?
Model Answer: The plastic material that is melted and deposited layer by layer to build an object.
