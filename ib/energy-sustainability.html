Energy Sustainability: Macro & Micro ⚡🌍

How the world powers up—big and small!

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Macro energy sustainability – Large-scale energy generation for nations or big communities (e.g., hydro, wind farms)
  • Micro energy sustainability – Small-scale energy generation for homes or small communities (e.g., rooftop solar)
  • Non-exhaustive sources – Energy sources that won't run out (e.g., sun, wind, water)
  • Energy security – Reliable, affordable access to energy
  • Infrastructure – The physical systems (like power grids) needed to deliver energy
  • Leapfrogging – Skipping old tech and moving straight to new solutions (e.g., mobile phones instead of landlines)

Core Concepts 🧠

Macro Energy Sustainability

  • Large-scale, grid-level energy generation
  • Examples: hydroelectric dams, wind farms, geothermal plants
  • Supplies power to cities, regions, or countries

Micro Energy Sustainability

  • Small-scale, local energy generation
  • Examples: rooftop solar panels, small wind turbines, solar water heaters
  • Empowers individuals and small communities

Energy Security

  • Uninterrupted, affordable access to energy
  • Critical for economic and social stability

Infrastructure & Leapfrogging

  • Developed countries rely on massive power grids (macro)
  • Developing countries can leapfrog to micro solutions (like mobile phones vs. landlines)

Real-World Stories

  • "The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind": a student in Malawi built a wind turbine to power his farm
  • Many African communities skipped landlines and went straight to mobile phones—now doing the same with micro energy!

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Some solar panels can power a whole home—even in remote areas!
  • Leapfrogging tech saves money and speeds up development.
  • Micro energy is helping millions get electricity for the first time.
