Product Life Cycles 🔄📈

From launch to decline—how products live, grow, and fade! ⏳

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Product life cycle – The stages a product goes through: launch, growth, maturity, decline
  • Launch/Introduction – Product is introduced to the market
  • Growth – Sales and profits increase
  • Maturity – Sales slow, market is saturated
  • Decline – Sales and profits fall
  • Obsolescence – Product becomes outdated or no longer used
  • Planned obsolescence – Product is intentionally made to become outdated
  • Functional obsolescence – Product wears out or parts become unavailable
  • Technological obsolescence – New technology replaces old products
  • Market saturation – Most potential customers already own the product
  • Limited release – Special, rare versions to boost sales
  • Product range – Different versions, colors, or models to increase sales

Core Concepts 🧠

Stages of the Product Life Cycle

  • Pre-launch: Idea, design, prototyping, pre-production
  • Launch/Introduction: Product enters the market, marketing hype, pricing strategies
  • Growth: Sales rise, advertising, word of mouth, product changes to boost sales
  • Maturity: Sales slow, market saturation, price reductions, new versions/colors
  • Decline: Sales fall, price drops, product may be discontinued

Obsolescence

  • Process of becoming outdated or no longer used
  • Examples: cassette tapes, VHS players

Planned Obsolescence

  • Product is intentionally made to become outdated
  • Example: Apple stops updating old iPhones

Functional Obsolescence

  • Product wears out or parts become unavailable
  • Example: Old car parts no longer made

Technological Obsolescence

  • New technology replaces old products
  • Example: Streaming replaces CDs, iPods replace Walkmans

Extending Product Life

  • New versions, colors, or models (e.g., Post-its in many colors)
  • Limited releases (e.g., rare sneakers, LOL dolls)
  • Product ranges for different markets (e.g., Toyota Corolla vs. Camry)

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • 3M gave away Post-its to create demand—now they come in every color!
  • Apple uses planned obsolescence to keep customers upgrading.
  • VHS players and cassette tapes were once essential—now they're obsolete.
  • Limited releases make products ultra-desirable (and collectible)!
