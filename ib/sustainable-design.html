Sustainable Design 🌱🛠️

Designing for people, planet, and profit! 🌍💡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Sustainable design – Designing products/services for social, economic, and environmental sustainability
  • Triple bottom line – People, planet, and profit (social, environmental, economic)
  • Green design – Design focused on minimizing environmental impact
  • Cradle to cradle – Product life cycle designed for continuous reuse/recycling
  • Incremental change – Small, step-by-step improvements
  • Radical change – Major, fundamental redesign
  • Bioplastics – Plastics made from renewable, plant-based sources
  • Dashevsky's five principles – Cyclic, solar, safe, efficient, social

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Sustainable Design?

  • Designing for social, economic, and environmental sustainability
  • Triple bottom line: people, planet, profit

Sustainable vs. Green Design

  • Green design: focuses on minimizing environmental impact (cradle to grave)
  • Sustainable design: considers environment, economy, and society (cradle to cradle)

Cradle to Cradle

  • Products designed for continuous reuse or recycling
  • Uses renewable resources, closes the loop

Incremental vs. Radical Change

  • Incremental: small, step-by-step improvements (e.g., switching to bioplastics)
  • Radical: major redesign for full sustainability (e.g., new materials, new systems)

Bioplastics Example

  • Made from plants (e.g., corn, sugarcane) instead of oil
  • Can be composted and renewed—more sustainable than petroleum plastics

Dashevsky's Five Principles

  • Cyclic: Use recycled/compostable materials, close the loop
  • Solar: Use renewable energy for manufacturing and use
  • Safe: Non-toxic, non-polluting byproducts
  • Efficient: Less energy, materials, and water than older products
  • Social: Uphold human rights, fair trade, safe work, and justice

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Bioplastics can be made from potatoes, corn, or sugarcane!
  • Cradle to cradle design aims for zero waste—everything is reused or recycled.
  • Dashevsky's principles help designers think beyond just "green" to true sustainability.
