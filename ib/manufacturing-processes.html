Manufacturing Processes: Additive, Subtractive, and More 🛠️⚙️

From 3D printing to laser cutting—manufacturing makes ideas real! ✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Design for manufacture – Designing products to suit available manufacturing processes
  • Additive manufacturing – Building up material to create a part (e.g., 3D printing)
  • Subtractive manufacturing – Removing material to create a part (e.g., cutting, machining)
  • CNC – Computer Numeric Control, automated machine tool control
  • CAM – Computer Aided Machining/Manufacturing
  • Cutting – Removing material with tools (saws, scissors, plasma, laser, water jet)
  • Sawing – Cutting with a saw
  • Scissors – Cutting with hand or power scissors
  • Planer – Machine for smoothing or shaping wood
  • Router – Tool for hollowing out or shaping edges
  • Sureform – Tool like a cheese grater for shaping
  • Spokeshave – Tool for shaping and smoothing wood
  • Hand planer – Manual tool for smoothing wood
  • Plasma cutter – Tool using superheated gas to cut metal
  • Oxyacetylene – Torch using oxygen and acetylene gas for cutting
  • Laser cutter – Machine using laser to cut materials
  • Water jet cutter – Machine using high-pressure water (and abrasives) to cut
  • Abrasive – Material for wearing away surfaces (sandpaper, grinding)
  • Sanding – Smoothing with abrasive paper or machines
  • Grinding – Wearing away material with a rotating abrasive
  • Machining – Shaping material with machines (drill press, mill, lathe, shaper)
  • Drill press – Machine for drilling vertical holes
  • Mill – Machine for cutting and shaping material
  • Lathe – Machine for shaping by rotating the workpiece
  • Shaper – Machine for cutting with a linear motion
  • Accuracy – Precision of a process or machine
  • Automation – Use of machines to perform tasks with minimal human input
  • Training – Learning to use specialized equipment
  • Capital investment – Money spent on expensive equipment

Core Concepts 🧠

Design for Manufacture

Designers must consider available manufacturing processes when creating products.
Example: Don't design for injection molding if you only have a 3D printer!

Additive vs. Subtractive Manufacturing

  • Additive: Builds up material (e.g., 3D printing, stereolithography)
  • Subtractive: Removes material (e.g., cutting, machining, sanding)

Cutting Techniques

  • Manual: Saws, scissors, planers, routers, sureform, spokeshave, hand planer
  • Powered: Plasma cutter, oxyacetylene torch, laser cutter, water jet cutter
  • CNC: Computer-controlled cutting (laser, plasma, water jet)
  • Pros: High accuracy, fine detail, less distortion
  • Cons: Expensive, needs training

Machining

  • Drill press: Drills vertical holes
  • Mill: Cuts and shapes in X, Y, Z axes
  • Lathe: Rotates workpiece for round shapes
  • Shaper: Cuts with linear motion
  • CNC/CAM: Automated, highly accurate, can run 24/7
  • Pros: High accuracy, beautiful finishes, less labor
  • Cons: Expensive, needs training, fewer jobs for skilled workers

Abrading

  • Sanding: Smoothing with abrasive paper or machines
  • Grinding: Wearing away with a rotating abrasive
  • Angle grinder, palm sander, belt sander: Common abrasive tools

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Water jet cutters can slice through stone and metal!
  • Laser cutters in schools cost $25,000+.
  • CNC machines can run 24/7 with little human help.

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between additive and subtractive manufacturing?
Model Answer: Additive manufacturing builds up material (like 3D printing); subtractive manufacturing removes material (like cutting or machining).
Why are CNC machines so accurate?

What's a disadvantage of automation in manufacturing?
Model Answer: Fewer jobs for skilled workers, and high capital investment in machines.
