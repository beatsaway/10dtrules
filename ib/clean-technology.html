Clean Technology 🌱💡

Why does it matter? Clean tech means less waste, less pollution, and a healthier planet for everyone! 🌍✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Clean technology 🌱 – Products, services, or processes that reduce waste and use fewer non-renewable resources
  • Service 💻 – Non-physical product (like banking, streaming, food delivery)
  • Process 🏭 – How something is made or done (manufacturing, energy generation)
  • Legislation 📜 – Laws that require or encourage clean tech
  • Community pressure 🤝 – When people or groups push for cleaner practices
  • International treaty 🌐 – Agreements between countries to protect the environment
  • Vision 2030 🇸🇦 – Saudi Arabia's plan to diversify its economy and reduce oil dependence

What is Clean Technology? 🌱

Definition

Clean technology (clean tech) means products, services, or processes that reduce waste and use as little non-renewable resources as possible. It's all about making things better for people and the planet!

Products, Services, and Processes

  • Products: Solar panels, electric cars, reusable water bottles
  • Services: Online banking, streaming movies, food delivery apps
  • Processes: Recycling, green manufacturing, renewable energy generation

Historical Context ⏳

Human Impact on the Environment

Humans have changed the planet through pollution, resource use, and waste. Ecology connects to clean tech because it shows how our actions affect the environment.

Pollution & London's Air

London used to have some of the world's worst air pollution! The Clean Air Acts (1950s–60s) made a huge difference, saving lives and making the city healthier.
Fun fact: The "Great Smog" of 1952 killed thousands and led to these laws.

Drivers of Clean Technology 🚦

Legislation (Laws)

Governments pass laws to force or encourage companies to clean up their act.
Example: Factories must filter air and water pollution or face fines.
Enforcement matters! A law that isn't enforced is just a suggestion.

Community & Media Pressure

People want clean air, water, and safe neighborhoods. Local communities and groups like Greenpeace push for change. Bad press = bad sales!

International Treaties

Countries agree to reduce pollution together.
Examples: Kyoto Protocol (cut emissions below 1990 levels), Paris Agreement (set targets for greenhouse gas reduction).

Vision 2030 (Saudi Arabia)

Saudi Arabia's plan to move beyond oil by investing in tourism, finance, green energy, and more.
Fun fact: Diversifying helps countries avoid being stuck if one resource (like oil) loses value!

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • Solar panels and wind turbines are classic clean tech products!
  • Some companies use recycled ocean plastic to make sneakers and clothes.
  • Electric buses are rolling out in cities worldwide to cut air pollution.
