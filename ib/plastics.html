Plastics: Types, Properties, and Environmental Impact 🧴♻️

Plastics: convenient, clever, but complicated for our planet! 🌍

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Polymer – Large molecule made of repeating units (monomers)
  • Monomer – Small molecule that joins to form polymers
  • Plastic – Synthetic polymer, can be shaped
  • Thermoplastic – Plastic that can be reheated and reshaped
  • Thermoset – Plastic that cannot be reshaped after setting
  • Hydrocarbon – Molecule made of hydrogen and carbon (source for plastics)
  • Ethylene, Propylene – Hydrocarbons used to make plastics
  • Microplastics – Tiny plastic particles from breakdown of larger plastics
  • Recycling – Process of reusing materials to make new products
  • Resin code – Number code identifying plastic type for recycling
  • Ductile – Can be stretched into wire
  • Stiffness – Resistance to bending
  • Dimensional stability – Maintains shape/size under stress
  • ABS – Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (Lego, 3D printing)
  • PET – Polyethylene terephthalate (bottles, containers)
  • PVC – Polyvinyl chloride (pipes, cables)
  • HIPS – High impact polystyrene (toys, utensils)
  • Polyurethane – Thermoset (sponges, wheels, toys)
  • Urea formaldehyde – Thermoset (MDF, insulation)
  • Melamine – Thermoset (hard plates, laminates)
  • Epoxy resin – Thermoset (adhesives, coatings)

Core Concepts 🧠

What are Plastics?

Plastics are synthetic polymers—large molecules made of repeating monomers. They're light, strong, and resistant to biological activity.
Fun fact: Every piece of plastic ever made still exists!

Environmental Impact

Plastics are often single-use and take 400–500 years to break down. They become microplastics, polluting oceans and harming wildlife. Only about 20% are recycled.

Thermoplastics

  • Can be reheated and reshaped repeatedly
  • Linear chains with weak bonds between them
  • Examples: PP (polypropylene), PE (polyethylene), PET, ABS, HIPS, PVC
  • Ductile, low stiffness, easy to manufacture and recycle

Thermosets

  • Once set, cannot be reshaped (strong cross-links)
  • High stiffness and strength, but not recyclable
  • Examples: Polyurethane, Urea formaldehyde, Melamine, Epoxy resin

Recycling & Resin Codes

  • Thermoplastics are easier to recycle than thermosets
  • Resin codes identify plastic types for recycling
  • Sorting and cleaning are major challenges
  • Most plastics still end up in landfills

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Lego bricks are made from ABS plastic.
  • Only about 1/5 of plastic products are recycled.
  • Microplastics are found in oceans, rivers, and even the air!

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between thermoplastic and thermoset plastics?
Model Answer: Thermoplastics can be reheated and reshaped; thermosets cannot be reshaped after setting due to strong cross-links.
Why are microplastics a problem?
Model Answer: Microplastics pollute the environment, harm marine life, and are difficult to remove from ecosystems.
Why are thermoplastics easier to recycle than thermosets?
Model Answer: Thermoplastics can be melted and reshaped, while thermosets cannot be remelted due to their strong cross-linked structure.
