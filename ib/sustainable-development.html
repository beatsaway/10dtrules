Sustainable Development 🌱🌍

Meeting today's needs without harming tomorrow! ♻️

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Sustainability – Long-term maintenance of responsibility (environmental, economic, social)
  • Sustainable development – Meeting present needs without compromising future generations
  • Triple bottom line – Economic, environmental, and social success
  • Environmental sustainability – Maintaining healthy ecosystems and biodiversity
  • Social sustainability – Maintaining local cultures, equity, and empowerment
  • Economic sustainability – Responsible growth, productivity, and efficiency
  • Decoupling – Disconnecting economic growth from environmental impact
  • Environmental regulation – Laws to protect the environment
  • Sustainability reporting – Company reports on economic, environmental, social, and governance performance
  • Product stewardship – Responsibility for environmental impact at all life cycle stages

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Sustainable Development?

  • Meets present needs without harming future generations
  • Balances environment, economy, and society

Triple Bottom Line

  • Success = economic + environmental + social
  • All three must be considered for true sustainability

Environmental Sustainability

  • Maintain healthy ecosystems and biodiversity
  • Respect nature's limits (e.g., chocolate vs. palm oil)

Social Sustainability

  • Empower local cultures and ensure equity
  • Prevent loss of livelihoods and cultural identity

Economic Sustainability

  • Grow responsibly, increase efficiency
  • Examples: efficient light bulbs, sustainable fisheries

Decoupling

  • Resource decoupling: use fewer resources for same output
  • Impact decoupling: less environmental harm as economy grows
  • Examples: LEED buildings, solar power plants

Environmental Regulations

  • Laws to limit pollution and protect nature
  • Example: rivers catching fire led to new regulations

Sustainability Reporting

  • Companies report on economic, environmental, social, and governance performance
  • Pros: morale, benchmarks, transparency, awareness
  • Cons: cost, data collection, possible image risk

Product Stewardship

  • Responsibility for environmental impact at all life cycle stages
  • Design, manufacture, use, and end-of-life (e.g., recycling, bottle deposits)

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Some rivers in the US caught fire due to pollution—now they're clean thanks to regulations!
  • LEED buildings are designed to use less energy and resources.
  • With bottle deposits, up to 80% of bottles get recycled (vs. 23% without deposits).
