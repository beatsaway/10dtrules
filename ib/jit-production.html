Just in Time (JIT) Production ⏱️🏭

How do companies minimize storage and maximize flexibility? 🚚

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Just in Time (JIT) – Production system where materials/components are ordered and products made only as needed
  • Cell Production – Small teams (cells) responsible for different parts of the process
  • Supply Chain – Network of suppliers and logistics for production
  • Cash Flow – Money available for use, not tied up in inventory
  • Flexibility – Ability to adapt production quickly

What is JIT? ❓

Definition

  • Materials/components ordered only when needed
  • Products made to order, not stored
  • Production split into cells (teams)

Advantages ✅

Lower Storage/Warehousing Costs

  • No need for large storage areas

Requires Less Staffing & Security

  • Fewer employees needed for storage/admin

Less Wastage & Overstock

  • Reduces waste, especially for perishables

Frees Up Cash Flow

  • Money not tied up in inventory

Highly Flexible

  • Easy to set up for short runs or changes

Disadvantages ⚠️

No Economy of Scale

  • Can't get bulk discounts

Supply Chain Risk

  • Any delay or failure can halt production

Hard to React to Demand

  • Not easy to ramp up quickly if demand spikes

Increased Transport Costs

  • Frequent small deliveries cost more

Distribution Delays

  • Customers may wait for products to be made

Fun Facts 🤓

  • JIT was popularized by Toyota in Japan to reduce costs and increase efficiency.
  • COVID-19 exposed JIT's vulnerability to supply chain disruptions (e.g., face masks).
