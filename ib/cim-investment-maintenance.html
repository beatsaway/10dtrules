CIM: Investment, Maintenance & Production Scales 💸🛠️

What does it take to run a computer-integrated factory? What are the costs and benefits? 🏭

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • CAD – Computer-Aided Design: designing and testing products virtually
  • Planning – Organizing production using computer data
  • Purchasing – Automated buying of materials/components
  • Cost Accounting – Budgeting and tracking costs with computers
  • Inventory Control – Tracking materials/products, often just-in-time
  • Distribution – Managing delivery and storage with computers
  • Investment – Money spent on robots, computers, and training
  • Maintenance – Keeping machines running, often by skilled workers
  • Training – Teaching workers to use and maintain advanced systems

Elements of CIM 🧩

CAD & Planning

  • Design and test products virtually
  • Plan production using data from design/testing

Purchasing & Inventory

  • Computers order materials/components automatically
  • Track inventory and use just-in-time methods

Cost Accounting & Distribution

  • Automated budgeting, receipts, and pricing
  • Computers manage delivery and storage

Investment & Maintenance 💰🛠️

Investment

  • High initial cost for robots, computers, and software
  • Long-term savings from efficiency and lower labor costs

Maintenance

  • Requires highly skilled workers
  • Computers can detect breakdowns early, reducing costs

Training

  • Workers need advanced training in computers and engineering
  • Training is expensive but necessary for modern factories

CIM & Production Scales 📏

One-off Production

  • Not cost-effective for custom/small runs

Batch & Mass Production

  • Best suited for batch/mass production
  • Flexible, efficient, and consistent

Fun Facts 💡

  • Some factories run 24/7 with almost no human workers!
  • Modern car mechanics often need computer science skills.
