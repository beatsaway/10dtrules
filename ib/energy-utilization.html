Energy Utilization ⚡🌍

Why does it matter? Understanding how we use, store, and distribute energy helps us design products that are efficient and sustainable! 🌱

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Energy utilization ⚡ – How energy is used, stored, and distributed
  • Embodied energy 🔋 – Total energy used to make, transport, use, and dispose of a product
  • Carbon footprint 🌫️ – Total greenhouse gases produced by a product or activity
  • Renewable energy 🌞 – Energy from sources that are naturally replenished (solar, wind, hydro)
  • Non-renewable energy ⛽ – Energy from sources that can run out (coal, oil, gas, nuclear)
  • Cradle to grave 🔄 – The full life cycle of a product, from creation to disposal
  • Megajoule (MJ) 💥 – A unit of energy (1 million joules)
  • kg CO₂/kg 🌫️ – Kilograms of carbon dioxide per kilogram of material

What is Energy Utilization? ⚡

Definition

Energy utilization is how energy is used, stored, and distributed in products and systems. It includes how we generate, store, and move energy to where it's needed.

Why It Matters

Efficient energy use reduces pollution, saves money, and helps fight climate change. Every product has an energy "footprint" from creation to disposal.

Energy Generation 🔋

Renewable Energy 🌞

Comes from sources that are naturally replenished, like solar, wind, and hydroelectric power.

Non-renewable Energy ⛽

Comes from sources that can run out, like coal, oil, gas, and nuclear power.

Embodied Energy & Carbon 🔋🌫️

Embodied Energy

The total energy required to produce a product, from raw material extraction to disposal. Measured in megajoules per kilogram (MJ/kg).

Example: Aluminum: 155 MJ/kg; Copper: 42 MJ/kg

Embodied Carbon

The total greenhouse gases (mainly CO₂) released during a product's life cycle. Measured in kg CO₂ per kg of material.

Example: 1 kg of aluminum = 8.24 kg CO₂

How to Calculate Embodied Energy

Multiply the energy per kg by the mass of the product.
Example: 18 kg of copper × 42 MJ/kg = 756 MJ

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • LED lights use up to 80% less energy than incandescent bulbs!
  • Electric cars have higher embodied energy at first, but lower over their lifetime compared to gas cars.
  • Solar panels pay back their embodied energy in 1–4 years of use.
