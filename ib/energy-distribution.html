Energy Distribution 🔌🌐

Why does it matter? How we move and use energy shapes our world, from powering cities to driving cars! 🚗⚡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Power grid 🌐 – Network for delivering electricity from producers to consumers
  • Local generation 🏠 – Producing energy close to where it's used
  • CHP (Combined Heat & Power) 🔥⚡ – Plants that generate both heat and electricity for local use
  • Embodied carbon 🌫️ – Total greenhouse gases released during a product's life cycle
  • Greenhouse gas 🌍 – Gases that trap heat in the atmosphere (CO₂, methane, etc.)
  • Global warming potential (GWP) 🔥 – How much a gas contributes to global warming compared to CO₂

How is Energy Distributed? 🔌

The Power Grid

The power grid is a huge network that delivers electricity from power plants to homes and businesses. It connects many sources and users, making energy available almost everywhere.

Watch: How the Power Grid Works (YouTube)

Local Generation

Energy can also be produced close to where it's used, like with solar panels on homes or small local power plants. This can make energy supply more reliable and reduce losses.

Watch: Local Power Generation (YouTube)

CHP (Combined Heat & Power)

CHP plants generate both heat and electricity for local communities, making energy use more efficient by capturing heat that would otherwise be wasted.

Watch: Combined Heat & Power (YouTube)

Case Study: Gas vs. Electric Cars 🚗⚡

Gas-Powered Cars

  • Quick to refuel, cheap to buy, lots of infrastructure
  • Higher maintenance, less environmentally friendly
  • Relies on fossil fuels (petrol/gasoline)

Electric Cars

  • Cheaper to maintain, more environmentally friendly
  • Slower to charge, higher upfront cost (but dropping!)
  • Can use renewable energy for charging

Embodied Carbon & Greenhouse Gases 🌫️🌍

Embodied Carbon

The total greenhouse gases released during a product's life cycle, from making it to using and disposing of it.

Greenhouse Gases & GWP

CO₂ is the most common greenhouse gas, but others like methane and nitrous oxide are much more potent. Global Warming Potential (GWP) compares their impact to CO₂ (GWP = 1).

Example: Methane GWP ≈ 25, Nitrous tetrafluoride GWP ≈ 17,000!

Carbon Emissions by Sector

Electricity production, transportation, and agriculture are the biggest sources of global greenhouse gas emissions. Reducing emissions in these sectors has a huge impact!

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • Electric cars can be charged at home using solar panels!
  • Some greenhouse gases stay in the atmosphere for thousands of years.
  • Combined Heat & Power (CHP) can make local energy use up to 80% efficient.
