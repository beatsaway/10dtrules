Innovation & Diffusion 🚀🌍

How do inventions become world-changing innovations? 💡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Innovation – An invention that is a commercial success
  • Marketability – How well a product can be sold
  • Financial support – Funding needed to bring a product to market
  • Marketing – Promoting and selling a product
  • Price – Cost to the consumer; must be affordable for the target market
  • Resistance to change – Reluctance to adopt new ideas/products
  • Risk aversion – Hesitancy to try new things due to uncertainty
  • Need – Demand for the invention
  • Disruptive innovation – New tech that challenges existing companies
  • Sustaining innovation – Improvements to existing products
  • Process innovation – New/improved manufacturing methods
  • Architectural innovation – New configuration of existing components
  • Modular innovation – Change to one or more key components
  • Radical innovation – Completely new idea/system/product
  • Configurational innovation – Change to both technology and organization
  • Diffusion – How a new idea/product spreads in the market
  • Suppression – When adoption of a new idea/product is slowed or blocked

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Innovation?

  • An invention that is a commercial success
  • Must make money and be accepted in the marketplace
  • Not all inventions become innovations!

Why Do Inventions Fail?

  • Poor marketability (not appealing or needed)
  • Lack of financial support
  • Weak marketing
  • Wrong price for target market
  • Resistance to change or risk aversion
  • No real need for the product

Types of Innovation

  • Disruptive: New tech that changes the market (e.g., smartphones)
  • Sustaining: Small improvements to existing products (e.g., new iPhone models)
  • Process: New/improved manufacturing methods (e.g., assembly line)
  • Architectural: New configuration of existing components (e.g., Walkman)
  • Modular: Change to key components (e.g., modular phones)
  • Radical: Completely new idea/system/product (e.g., bladeless fans)
  • Configurational: Change to both technology and organization

Diffusion

  • How quickly a new idea/product is accepted in the market
  • Can be increased by good marketing, clear need, affordability, etc.

Suppression

  • When adoption of a new idea/product is slowed or blocked
  • Can be due to legal issues, resistance, or competition

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Olestra chips were an invention, but not an innovation—they failed in the market!
  • The Ford Edsel is a classic example of a failed invention.
  • Apple's iPhone was a disruptive innovation that changed the world.
  • LaserDiscs never caught on—sometimes the market just isn't ready!

Quick Quiz ⚡

What is the difference between an invention and an innovation?
Model Answer: An invention is a new idea/product; an innovation is an invention that is a commercial success.
Why do most inventions fail to become innovations?
Model Answer: Poor marketability, lack of financial support, weak marketing, wrong price, resistance to change, risk aversion, or no real need.
What is disruptive innovation?
Model Answer: New technology that changes the market and challenges existing companies (e.g., smartphones).
