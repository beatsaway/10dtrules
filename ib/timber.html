Timber: Natural & Man-Made Woods 🌲🪵

From forests to furniture—timber builds our world! 🏡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Timber – Wood used as a material (organic, cellulose & lignin)
  • Cellulose – Protein in wood, strong in tension
  • Lignin – Protein in wood, strong in compression
  • Softwood – Timber from conifers/gymnosperms (e.g., pine, spruce)
  • Hardwood – Timber from deciduous/angiosperms (e.g., oak, walnut, balsa)
  • Tracheids – Fluid-transporting cells in softwood
  • Vessels & fibers – Fluid-transporting cells in hardwood
  • Grain – Pattern of growth rings in wood
  • With/against the grain – Direction of cut relative to grain
  • Janka hardness – Test for wood hardness (force to embed steel ball)
  • Knot – Defect from a branch in timber
  • Warp, cupping, bow, twist, crook – Types of wood deformation
  • Seasoning – Drying wood to remove water
  • Man-made wood – Engineered wood (plywood, glulam, MDF, particle board)
  • Dimensional stability – Resistance to changes in shape/size
  • Uniformity – Consistency of properties throughout material
  • Aesthetics – Visual appeal

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Timber?

Timber is wood used as a material, made of cellulose (strong in tension) and lignin (strong in compression).
Fun fact: Ancient forests formed coal beds we use today!

Natural Timber: Softwood vs Hardwood

Softwood: From conifers, mostly tracheids, e.g., pine, spruce, fir, cedar.
Hardwood: From angiosperms, vessels & fibers, e.g., oak, walnut, balsa.
Janka hardness: Measures force to embed a steel ball—balsa is a hardwood but softer than most softwoods!

Grain Structure

Wood grows in rings (grains).
With the grain: Along the rings.
Against the grain: Perpendicular to rings.
Grain affects strength and appearance.

Defects & Strength

Knots: Branches in timber.
Warps: Cupping, bow, twist, crook.
Seasoning: Drying wood increases strength.
Wet wood is easier to bend; dry wood is stronger.

Man-Made Woods

Plywood: Layers of wood glued with grains crossing.
Glulam: Laminated wood glued together.
MDF: Medium density fiberboard, uniform throughout.
Particle board: Chips of wood glued together.
Advantages: Dimensional stability, uniformity, product sizes.
Disadvantages: Aesthetics, water resistance, staining.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Coal beds formed from ancient forests in the Carboniferous period.
  • Balsa is a hardwood but softer than most softwoods!
  • Marine plywood is used for boats and resists water.

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between softwood and hardwood?
Model Answer: Softwood comes from conifers (gymnosperms), hardwood from angiosperms; structure, not actual hardness, is the main difference.
What is the Janka hardness test?
Model Answer: A test measuring the force needed to embed a steel ball halfway into wood.
What is dimensional stability?
Model Answer: The ability of a material to maintain its shape and size when exposed to changes in temperature and humidity.
