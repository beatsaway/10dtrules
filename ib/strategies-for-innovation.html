Strategies for Innovation 💡🔄

How do new ideas actually happen? ✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Active insight (Eureka moment) – Sudden realization or solution after thinking about a problem
  • Adaptation – Using a solution from one field in another (e.g., hovercraft → hover mower)
  • Technology transfer – Applying a tech advance from one area to another (e.g., radar → microwave oven)
  • Analogy – Using an idea from one context to solve a problem in another (e.g., bats → sonar)
  • Chance discovery – Unexpected finding that leads to a new idea (e.g., Velcro)
  • Technology push – New tech or research leads to new products (e.g., Post-it notes)
  • Market pull – Market demand drives invention (e.g., Sugru moldable rubber)

Core Concepts 🧠

Active Insight (Eureka Moment)

  • Sudden realization or solution after thinking about a problem
  • Often comes after a period of frustration or deep thought
  • Example: Archimedes and the crown

Adaptation

  • Solution from one field used in another
  • Example: Hovercraft principle used for hover mowers
  • Biomimicry: Kingfisher beak inspires bullet train design

Technology Transfer

  • Tech advance in one area applied to another
  • Example: Radar tech → microwave oven

Analogy

  • Idea from one context inspires solution in another
  • Example: Bats use echolocation → sonar for boats

Chance Discovery

  • Unexpected finding leads to new idea
  • Example: Velcro inspired by burrs sticking to clothes

Technology Push

  • New tech or research leads to new products
  • Example: Post-it notes from failed adhesive

Market Pull

  • Market demand drives invention
  • Example: Sugru moldable rubber invented to meet user needs

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Many "Eureka moments" come after lots of hard work!
  • Biomimicry: Nature inspires many inventions (e.g., kingfisher → bullet train)
  • Velcro was inspired by burrs sticking to socks in Switzerland.
  • Post-it notes were invented by accident!
