Computer-Aided Design (CAD) 💻🛠️

Design it, tweak it, make it real! CAD is the designer's superpower—turning ideas into digital reality. ✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • CAD – Computer-Aided Design: using software to create, modify, analyze, or optimize a design
  • 2D modeling – Flat drawings (length & width), e.g., Adobe Illustrator, AutoCAD
  • 3D modeling – Digital objects with length, width, and depth, e.g., Fusion 360, SketchUp, SolidWorks
  • Rendering – Making a 3D model look real with lighting, textures, and shadows (e.g., Blender, 3DS Max)
  • Surface model – Realistic image of a product's exterior, no interior data
  • Solid model – Includes both exterior and interior data (volume, structure)
  • Data model – Organizes and structures data, often for IT systems
  • Virtual prototyping – Interactive digital mock-ups for testing and feedback

Core Concepts 🧠

What is CAD?

CAD stands for Computer-Aided Design. It's software that helps you create, modify, and perfect your designs—faster and more accurately than by hand.

2D vs 3D Modeling

2D: Flat drawings (length & width). Examples: Adobe Illustrator, AutoCAD.
3D: Digital objects with depth. Examples: Fusion 360, SketchUp, SolidWorks.

Rendering

Rendering makes 3D models look real by adding lighting, textures, and shadows. Examples: Blender, 3DS Max.

Surface Model

Shows the outside of a product. Looks real, but has no info about the inside. Example: A mouse's exterior.

Solid Model

Includes both the outside and inside of a product—volume, structure, and all. Example: A cutaway of a mouse showing internal parts.

Data Model

Organizes and structures data, often for IT systems. Example: Database design for an app.

Virtual Prototyping

Interactive digital mock-ups for testing and feedback. Example: Simulating packaging in a virtual store.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • CAD is used in everything from sneakers to skyscrapers!
  • Blender is free and used by pros and students alike.
