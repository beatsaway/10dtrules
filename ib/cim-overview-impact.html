Computer Integrated Manufacturing (CIM): Overview & Impact 🤖🏭

How do computers run the modern factory? What does it mean for jobs and skills? 💻

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • CIM – Computer Integrated Manufacturing: using computers to integrate all aspects of production
  • Automation – Using machines/robots to perform tasks with little human input
  • Integration – Linking design, procurement, manufacturing, and distribution
  • Design – Using CAD and computers to create/test products
  • Procurement – Buying materials/components, often automated
  • Manufacturing – Making products, often with robots
  • Distribution – Delivering products, tracked by computers
  • Job Loss – Fewer jobs for humans, more for robots
  • Skills – Higher education/skills needed for modern manufacturing jobs

What is CIM? ❓

Definition

  • System using computers to integrate all production steps
  • From design to procurement, manufacturing, and distribution

Automation & Integration

  • Computers/robots handle tasks once done by humans
  • Processes are linked for efficiency

Impact on Jobs & Skills 👩‍💻🤖

Job Loss

  • Robots replace many manual jobs
  • Fewer people needed on the production line

Need for Higher Skills

  • Workers must understand computers, math, science
  • More education/training required

Advantages & Disadvantages ⚖️

Advantages

  • Greater efficiency and consistency
  • 24/7 operation, fewer errors, higher quality
  • Lower long-term costs

Disadvantages

  • High initial investment
  • Job loss, need for retraining
  • Less individuality in work

CIM & Scales of Production 📏

One-off Production

  • Not cost-effective for small/custom jobs

Batch & Mass Production

  • Highly suited for batch/mass production
  • Flexible, efficient, fewer errors, lower costs

Fun Facts 💡

  • Apple, Tesla, and Toyota all use CIM to streamline their factories.
  • Modern factory workers often need a university degree!
