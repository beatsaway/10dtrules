Textiles: Natural & Synthetic Fibers, and Fabric Making 🧵👗

Textiles: from ancient looms to modern fashion! 🪡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Textile – Any type of cloth or woven fabric
  • Fiber – Thin thread-like structure, natural or synthetic
  • Natural fiber – Fiber from plants, animals, or minerals
  • Synthetic fiber – Man-made fiber, usually from polymers
  • Hydrophilic – Attracted to water (easy to dye)
  • Hydrophobic – Repels water (hard to dye)
  • Resilience – Ability to recover shape after stretching
  • Dimensional stability – Maintains shape/size
  • Wool – Animal fiber from hair (sheep, alpaca, cashmere)
  • Cotton – Plant fiber from seed hairs
  • Linen – Plant fiber from flax phloem
  • Silk – Animal fiber from silkworms
  • Polyester – Synthetic fiber, durable, often blended
  • Nylon – Synthetic fiber, strong, flexible
  • Yarn – Long, continuous length of interlocked fibers
  • Knitting – Making fabric by interlocking loops of yarn
  • Weaving – Making fabric by interlacing threads at right angles
  • Lace – Decorative fabric with open patterns
  • Felting – Making fabric by matting fibers together
  • Warp – Threads running lengthwise in woven fabric
  • Weft – Threads woven across warp threads
  • Mildew – Fungus that can damage natural fibers

Core Concepts 🧠

What are Textiles?

Textiles are cloth or woven fabrics made from fibers.
Natural fibers: From plants, animals, or minerals.
Synthetic fibers: Man-made, usually from polymers.

Natural Fibers

  • Plant: Cotton (seed hairs), linen (flax phloem)
  • Animal: Wool (hair), silk (silkworms)
  • Mineral: Asbestos (rare, not used in clothing now)
  • Hydrophilic, easy to dye, flammable, break down over time, attacked by mildew

Synthetic Fibers

  • Nylon: Strong, flexible, hydrophobic, resists mildew, good resilience
  • Polyester: Durable, hydrophobic, often blended with cotton
  • Thermoplasticity (can be melted and reformed), not dimensionally stable, hard to dye

Making Yarn

Fibers are spun into yarn—a long, continuous length of interlocked fibers.
Fun fact: The flag of India features a spinning wheel!

Making Fabric

  • Knitting: Interlocking loops (warp and weft knitting)
  • Weaving: Interlacing threads at right angles (warp and weft)
  • Lace: Decorative, open patterns
  • Felting: Matting fibers together (oldest fabric type)

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • The flag of India features a spinning wheel (yarn-making symbol).
  • Polyester is everywhere—from car seats to couches.
  • Ancient textiles are rare because they break down easily.

Quick Quiz ⚡

What's the difference between hydrophilic and hydrophobic fibers?
Model Answer: Hydrophilic fibers attract water and are easy to dye; hydrophobic fibers repel water and are hard to dye.
Why are ancient textiles so rare?
Model Answer: Natural fibers break down easily due to bacteria, mildew, and environmental factors, so few survive from ancient times.
What is the difference between knitting and weaving?
Model Answer: Knitting interlocks loops of yarn; weaving interlaces threads at right angles (warp and weft).
