Usability Testing Environments 🏠🧪

Where you test matters as much as what you test! 🔬

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Natural environment – Real-world setting where a product is used
  • Usability laboratory – Controlled environment for testing products
  • Qualitative data – Descriptive, non-numerical information
  • Quantitative data – Measurable, numerical information
  • Observer bias – Influence of the observer's expectations on results
  • Sensors & tech – Tools like eye-tracking, cameras, and one-way mirrors

Core Concepts 🧠

Natural Environment

  • Testing in the real-world context of use
  • Authentic, reveals real user behavior
  • Mostly qualitative data

Usability Laboratory

  • Controlled, artificial environment
  • Allows for sensors, multiple observers, and repeatable tests
  • Mostly quantitative data

Advantages & Disadvantages

  • Natural: authentic, but less measurable and more observer bias
  • Lab: controlled, measurable, but costly and less natural

Qualitative vs. Quantitative Data

  • Qualitative: opinions, feelings, observations
  • Quantitative: times, error rates, scores

Observer Bias

  • Observers can unintentionally influence results
  • More likely in natural settings

Sensors & Tech

  • Eye-tracking, cameras, and one-way mirrors help collect data
  • Used mostly in labs for detailed analysis

Real-World Examples

  • Testing a kettle in a user's kitchen (natural)
  • Eye-tracking users on a website in a lab
  • Usability labs for apps, websites, and even fruit!

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Some usability labs use one-way mirrors so users can't see observers!
  • Eye-tracking tech can show exactly where users look on a screen.
  • Natural environment testing can reveal problems labs miss.
