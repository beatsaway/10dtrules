Innovation Design & Marketing Specifications 📊🛍️

How do you know if your design will succeed in the market? 💡

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Market analysis – Appraisal of the economic viability of a design from a market perspective
  • Target market – The specific group a product is aimed at
  • Market sector – Large division of a market (e.g., electronics, household, automotive)
  • Market segment – Smaller division based on consumer characteristics (e.g., age, gender, ethnicity)
  • User need – The need or problem a product solves for users
  • Consumer need – How consumers identify needs/wants when buying
  • Marketer need – Needs created or influenced by marketing (e.g., Listerine and halitosis)
  • New products – Innovations that create new needs or wants

Core Concepts 🧠

Market Analysis

  • Is there a market for your design?
  • Considers costs, pricing, and potential users
  • Techniques: sales data, Google Trends, questionnaires, focus groups, interviews

Target Market

  • The specific group your product is aimed at
  • Identified by market sector and segment

Market Sector & Segment

  • Sector: Large division (e.g., electronics, household, automotive)
  • Segment: Smaller group (e.g., age, gender, ethnicity)

User Need

  • Is there a real need for your product?
  • Considers demand, magnitude, profitability, perception, and IP issues

Consumer Need

  • How consumers identify needs/wants when buying
  • Out of stock, dissatisfaction, changing circumstances, related products, marketer need, new products

Marketer Need

  • Needs created or influenced by marketing
  • Example: Listerine invented "halitosis" (bad breath)!

New Products

  • Innovations can create new needs/wants
  • Example: Nobody needed an iPad until it existed!

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Thomas Edison said, "Anything that won't sell, I don't want to invent."
  • Market analysis can use Google Trends, but questionnaires and focus groups are more reliable.
  • Marketers have invented needs—like Listerine inventing "halitosis" (bad breath)!
  • New products often create needs we didn't know we had (e.g., iPads, smartphones).
