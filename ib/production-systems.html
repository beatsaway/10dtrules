Production Systems: Craft, Mechanized, Automated, and More 🏭🤖

From handmade rings to robot-built cars—production systems shape our world! 💍🚗

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Production system – Method for organizing manufacturing
  • Craft production – Small-scale, manual, one-off or batch production
  • Mechanized production – Volume production using machines controlled by humans
  • Automated production – Volume production using machines controlled by computers
  • Assembly line – Products move along a conveyor, parts added at each station
  • Mass production – Large volumes of standardized products
  • CNC – Computer Numeric Control, computer-controlled machines
  • G-code – Programming language for CNC machines
  • Feed speed – Rate at which material is fed into a machine
  • Mass customization – Mass production with individual customization
  • Computer integrated manufacturing – Systems linking design, production, and business
  • Design for manufacture – Designing for available manufacturing capabilities
  • Design for materials – Choosing materials available for production
  • Design for process – Designing for a specific manufacturing process
  • Design for assembly – Designing to simplify assembly
  • Design for disassembly – Designing for easy disassembly and recycling
  • Flexibility – Ability to adapt production to different products
  • Labor market – Supply and demand for workers
  • Economies of scale – Cost advantages from large-scale production
  • Value – Worth of a product or process
  • Interchangeable parts – Standardized parts that can be swapped
  • Disassembly – Taking products apart for repair or recycling

Core Concepts 🧠

What are Production Systems?

Production systems are ways of organizing manufacturing.
Selection criteria: Economy of scale, value, labor, flexibility, environment.

Craft Production

Small-scale, manual, one-off or batch production.
Examples: Custom jewelry, handmade furniture.
Pros: High quality, flexible, skilled labor.
Cons: Expensive, slow, no economies of scale.

Mechanized Production

Volume production using machines controlled by humans.
Examples: Shipbuilding, machine shops.
Pros: More efficient than craft, skilled labor.
Cons: Still expensive, needs skilled workers.

Automated Production

Volume production using machines controlled by computers.
Examples: CNC machining, automated factories.
Pros: High precision, lower labor costs, high volume.
Cons: High machine cost, less flexibility, less skilled labor needed.

Assembly Lines & Mass Production

Products move along a conveyor, parts added at each station.
Examples: Car factories, Lego factories.
Pros: High volume, low cost per unit, interchangeable parts.
Cons: Less customization, high capital investment, less motivated workers.

CNC & Mass Customization

CNC: Computer-controlled machines using G-code for precise manufacturing.
Mass customization: Mass production with individual customization (e.g., custom sneakers, cars).

Design for Manufacture

  • Design for materials: Choose materials you have available.
  • Design for process: Design for the manufacturing process you can use.
  • Design for assembly: Simplify assembly, reduce parts.
  • Design for disassembly: Make products easy to take apart for repair or recycling.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Custom jewelry is craft production; Lego factories are mass production.
  • CNC machines use G-code to make complex parts.
  • Mass customization lets you design your own sneakers or cars online!
