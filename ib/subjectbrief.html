⚡ What is this?

IB Design Tech is where you learn to solve real problems creatively and practically. You'll design things that matter, think outside the box, and build skills that actually help in the real world. It's a mix of engineering, creativity, and making life better for people.


What is it like as a subject? Less memorizing, more doing. You'll tackle real challenges, build and test your ideas, learn from mistakes, and keep improving until it works.

🎯 What You'll Actually Learn

This isn't your typical "sit and memorize" subject. You're going to develop design thinking - basically a way of thinking to look at any problem and figuring out how to solve it.

The Design Thinking Process:

  • Empathize: Actually understand what people need (not what you think they need)
  • Define: Turn messy problems into clear challenges
  • Ideate: Brainstorm like your life depends on it
  • Prototype: Build quick, rough versions to test ideas
  • Test: See if your solution actually works in the real world

📚 Course Breakdown

The course is split into three main areas that actually make sense:

🧠 Design in Theory (A)

  • How humans interact with stuff (ergonomics)
  • Research methods that don't suck
  • Prototyping techniques
  • Materials and how they work
  • Structural, mechanical & electronic systems (HL)
  • Manufacturing techniques (HL)

⚙️ Design in Practice (B)

  • User-centred design (putting people first)
  • The IB Design process
  • Modeling and prototyping for real
  • Choosing the right materials
  • System applications (HL)
  • Production systems (HL)

🌍 Design in Context (C)

  • Designer responsibility (with great power...)
  • Inclusive design (design for everyone)
  • Sustainable design
  • Circular economy principles
  • Product analysis and evaluation
  • Life cycle analysis (HL)

🛠️ The Main Project (Internal Assessment)

Here's where it gets real. You'll spend a big chunk of your time on a Design Project where you:

  • Find a real problem that actually matters to someone
  • Design a solution that could genuinely work
  • Build and test prototypes (prepare for lots of iterations)
  • Work with real users to see if your solution is any good
  • Document everything like the professional designer you're becoming

This isn't busy work - this is you actually solving problems that matter. Some students have created apps, medical devices, sustainable products, and solutions for their local communities.
