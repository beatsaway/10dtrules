Advanced CAD Technologies 🤖🕹️

From digital humans to VR, advanced CAD brings imagination to life! 🚀

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Bottom-up design – Creating all parts separately, then assembling (e.g., piston)
  • Top-down design – Designing the overall shape first, then fitting components inside (e.g., car body)
  • Digital humans – Computer models simulating human mechanics/biology for testing (e.g., pilot reach)
  • Motion capture – Recording real human movement for animation (e.g., Andy Serkis as Gollum)
  • Haptic technology – Tech that simulates touch/force feedback (e.g., VR suits, game controllers)
  • Virtual reality (VR) – Simulating real situations on screen, interactively
  • Animation – Linking graphics to simulate motion or process
  • Finite element analysis (FEA) – Simulating stresses/forces in products using CAD

Core Concepts 🧠

Bottom-Up vs Top-Down Design

Bottom-up: Build all parts first, then assemble (e.g., piston).
Top-down: Design the outside first, then fit components inside (e.g., car body).

Digital Humans

Computer models that simulate human mechanics and biology. Used for testing ergonomics, reach, and comfort (e.g., airline pilot controls).

Motion Capture

Recording real human movement for animation. Example: Andy Serkis as Gollum or Planet of the Apes.

Haptic Technology

Simulates touch and force feedback. Examples: VR suits, game controllers, Ready Player One suit.

Virtual Reality (VR)

Simulates real situations on screen and lets you interact naturally. Example: Running in a VR environment with a headset.

Animation

Linking graphics/screens to simulate motion or process. Example: Animating a product to show how it works.

Finite Element Analysis (FEA)

Simulates stresses and forces in products using CAD. Example: Analyzing where a bike frame might break under stress.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Andy Serkis is the king of motion capture (Gollum, Planet of the Apes).
  • Haptic suits are real and used for VR gaming and professional training.
