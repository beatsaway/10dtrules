Green Design 🌱🛠️

Why does it matter? Green design means making products that are better for people and the planet—throughout their whole life! 🌍✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Green design 🌱 – Designing with environmental impact in mind, from creation to disposal
  • Efficiency ⚡ – Using materials, energy, and resources wisely
  • Pollution 💨 – Harmful substances released into the environment
  • Product life cycle 🔄 – The journey of a product from creation to disposal
  • Compostable 🍃 – Can break down naturally and return to the earth
  • Labeling 🏷️ – Marking products to help with recycling or disposal

What is Green Design? 🌱

Definition

Green design is all about creating products that consider their environmental impact at every stage—from materials and manufacturing to use and disposal. It's about making things better for the planet and for people!

Objectives of Green Design 🎯

Increase Efficiency

Use less material, energy, and resources.
Example: LED bulbs use less energy than old-school bulbs!

Minimize Waste & Pollution

Choose materials and processes that create less waste and pollution.
Example: PLA plastic (from corn) is compostable, unlike ABS plastic.

Reduce Long-Term Harm

Design to avoid lasting damage to the environment.
Example: Prevent toxic chemicals from leaking into rivers or water tables.

Appropriate Product Life

Make products last as long as needed—no more, no less.
Example: Reusable cloth bags vs. single-use plastic bags.

Consider End-of-Life

Plan for recycling, composting, or safe disposal.
Example: Minimize packaging, use recyclable materials, design for disassembly.

Eco-Friendly Packaging

Use packaging that breaks down quickly or can be recycled.
Example: Paper cups instead of polystyrene cups.

Minimize Noise & Smell

Reduce unpleasant odors and loud sounds from products or factories.
Example: Factories with filters and sound barriers.

Reduce Safety Hazards

Follow the safety pyramid: eliminate, substitute, isolate, control, use PPE.
Example: Safety goggles, emergency stop buttons, and safer machines.

Minimize Materials

Use fewer types of materials to make recycling easier.
Example: Avoid complex composites like Tetra Pak cartons.

Clear Labeling

Label products so they can be sorted and recycled properly.
Example: Recycling codes on plastics.

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • Single-use plastic bags are used for an average of just 12 minutes!
  • Some safety equipment can instantly stop a saw if it touches skin—amazing tech!
  • Polystyrene cups can last 500+ years in a landfill. Paper cups? Just a few months.
