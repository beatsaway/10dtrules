Just in Case (JIC) Production 📦🏭

How do companies prepare for uncertainty and demand spikes? 📈

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Just in Case (JIC) – Production system where materials/components are stockpiled and products are made in advance
  • Overstock – Excess inventory that may not be sold
  • Supply Chain – Network of suppliers and logistics for production
  • Economies of Scale – Cost advantages from buying/producing in bulk
  • Flexibility – Ability to adapt production quickly (JIC is less flexible)

What is JIC? ❓

Definition

  • Materials/components stockpiled in advance
  • Products made and stored until needed
  • Prepares for supply chain disruptions or demand spikes

Advantages ✅

Cheaper Prices (Economies of Scale)

  • Bulk buying reduces per-unit cost

Supply Chain Resilience

  • Easy to weather failures in the supply chain

Easier to React to Demand

  • Can meet sudden increases in demand

Lower Transport Costs

  • Bulk shipments reduce transport costs

Fast Distribution

  • No delay—products are ready to ship

Disadvantages ⚠️

Higher Storage/Warehousing Costs

  • Need large storage areas, more staff/security

Overstock & Wastage

  • Risk of unsellable or perishable goods, theft

Tied-up Capital

  • Money tied up in inventory, not available for other uses

Less Flexibility

  • Harder to change production lines for new products

More Staffing/Admin Needed

  • More employees needed for storage/admin

Fun Facts 🤓

  • JIC is common in industries with unpredictable demand or long supply chains (e.g., supermarkets, electronics).
  • COVID-19 showed the value of JIC for critical goods like PPE and ventilators.
