Sustainable Consumption 🌿🛒

Consuming smarter, not just less! ♻️

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Sustainable consumption – Using goods/services with minimal environmental impact, social equity, and economic viability
  • Eco-warrior – Actively demonstrates for environmental issues
  • Eco-fan – Enthusiastically adopts eco-friendly practices
  • Eco-champion – Champions environmental issues within an organization
  • Ecophobe – Resents or opposes environmental protection
  • Eco-labeling – Labels showing a product is environmentally friendly
  • Energy labeling – Labels showing energy efficiency and consumption
  • Take-back program – Company takes back products at end of life for recycling
  • Just-in-time manufacturing – Producing goods only as needed, reducing waste
  • Pressure group – Organization promoting positive change (e.g., WWF, Greenpeace)

Core Concepts 🧠

What is Sustainable Consumption?

  • Consuming goods/services with minimal environmental impact
  • Focus on smarter, not just less, consumption

Consuming Differently

  • Less packaging, eco-labels, energy-efficient products, take-back programs
  • Not just reducing, but making better choices

Eco-Warriors, Eco-Fans, Eco-Champions, Ecophobes

  • Eco-warrior: Actively demonstrates for the environment
  • Eco-fan: Adopts eco-friendly habits
  • Eco-champion: Promotes sustainability in organizations
  • Ecophobe: Opposes environmental protection

Eco-Labeling & Energy Labeling

  • Eco-labels: Show products are environmentally friendly
  • Energy labels: Show efficiency, consumption, and other details

Reading Energy Labels

  • Look for stars, kilowatt hours/year, efficiency ratings (A+++, etc.)
  • Lower kWh = more efficient

Company Strategies

  • Design brief, pricing, demand, production, just-in-time, take-back, radical changes
  • Consider value for money, long-term costs, and sustainability

Pressure Groups

  • Organizations like WWF, Greenpeace push for sustainable change
  • Influence policy, design, and consumer behavior

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Eco-labels can be found on everything from paper to electronics to food!
  • Energy labels in the EU use A+++ (best) to D (worst) ratings—green is good, red is bad.
  • Just-in-time manufacturing reduces waste and storage costs.
