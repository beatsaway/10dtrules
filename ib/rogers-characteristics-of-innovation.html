Rogers' Characteristics of Innovation & Consumers 🌟🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Why do people adopt new ideas? Rogers explains how innovation spreads! 🚀

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Relative advantage – Is the new product better than what came before?
  • Compatibility – Does it fit with users' values, needs, and practices?
  • Complexity – Is it easy or hard to use?
  • Observability – Can people see the results of the innovation?
  • Trialability – Can people try it before committing?
  • Conspicuous consumption – Buying things to be seen by others
  • Social roots of consumerism – How groups, values, and lifestyles affect adoption

Core Concepts 🧠

Relative Advantage

  • Is the innovation better than what came before?
  • Example: Washing machines vs. hand washing
  • More advantage = more likely to be adopted

Compatibility

  • Does it fit with users' values, needs, and practices?
  • Example: Organic food for people who value natural products

Complexity

  • Is it easy or hard to use?
  • Less complex = more likely to be adopted
  • Example: Apple products are designed to feel simple

Observability

  • Can people see the results of the innovation?
  • Example: AirPods, solar panels on houses
  • More visible = more likely to be adopted

Trialability

  • Can people try it before committing?
  • Example: Test driving a car, trying devices at the Apple Store

Social Roots of Consumerism

  • Groups, values, and lifestyles affect adoption
  • Example: Techies adopt early, vegans avoid meat substitutes

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Everett Rogers published his theory in 1962—it's still used today!
  • Some people put solar panels on the wrong side of their house just to be seen!
  • Apple Stores let you try everything—boosting trialability and adoption.
  • Test drives are a classic way to increase trialability for cars.

Quick Quiz ⚡

What are Rogers' five characteristics of innovation adoption?
Model Answer: Relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, observability, trialability.
Give an example of observability in innovation.
Model Answer: AirPods or solar panels—people can see them, which encourages adoption.
Why is trialability important for new products?
Model Answer: If people can try a product before buying, they're more likely to adopt it (e.g., test drives, Apple Store demos).
