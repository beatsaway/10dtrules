Additive Manufacturing Techniques 🧩🖨️

Layer by layer, additive manufacturing builds the future! 🏗️

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Stereolithography (SLA) – Additive process using UV laser to harden resin layer by layer
  • Laminated Object Manufacturing (LOM) – Laser cuts and stacks sheets of material to build up an object
  • Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) – 3D printing technique that melts and deposits layers of material (e.g., plastic, concrete)
  • Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) – Laser fuses powder particles into a solid 3D shape
  • Filament – Material (often plastic) melted in FDM 3D printing
  • Resin – Liquid material hardened by light in SLA 3D printing
  • Powder – Fine material fused by laser in SLS
  • Layers – Thin slices added one at a time to build up a 3D object

Core Concepts 🧠

Stereolithography (SLA)

Additive process using a UV laser to harden liquid resin at specific points, building up an object layer by layer.

Laminated Object Manufacturing (LOM)

Laser cuts sheets of material, which are then stacked and bonded to form a 3D object. Like stacking and gluing laser-cut wood layers!

Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM)

3D printing technique that melts and deposits material (like plastic or concrete) in layers. Used for everything from phone cases to houses!

Selective Laser Sintering (SLS)

Laser fuses together tiny powder particles to form a solid 3D shape. Great for complex, strong parts.

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • 3D printed houses are now a thing—giant printers use concrete in FDM style!
  • Students have made phone stands by stacking laser-cut wood (like LOM).
