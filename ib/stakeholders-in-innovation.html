Stakeholders in Innovation 🤝💡

Who makes innovation happen? 🌟

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Inventor – Person who creates a new idea or product
  • Product champion – Influential individual who pushes an idea forward within an organization
  • Entrepreneur – Person who takes an invention to market, often by financing and developing it
  • Multidisciplinary team – Group with diverse expertise working together on innovation
  • Discipline – Area of knowledge or expertise (e.g., chemistry, marketing)
  • Cross-fertilization – Sharing ideas between people with different backgrounds
  • Groupthink – When everyone in a group thinks alike, limiting creativity

Core Concepts 🧠

Inventors

  • Create new ideas or products
  • Examples: Edison, Tesla, Ford, Da Vinci, Wright brothers, Marconi, Babbage
  • Primary stakeholders in innovation

Product Champions

  • Influential individuals in organizations
  • Push ideas forward (e.g., Steve Jobs, Elon Musk)
  • May not be the original inventor

Entrepreneurs

  • Take inventions to market
  • Finance, develop, and diffuse products
  • Often work outside large organizations
  • Example: Inventor of Sugru

Multidisciplinary Teams

  • Groups with diverse expertise (e.g., engineering, marketing, law)
  • Most modern products require teamwork
  • Enable cross-fertilization of ideas

Advantages & Disadvantages of Multidisciplinary Teams

  • Advantages: Wide range of knowledge, diverse perspectives, cross-fertilization, less groupthink
  • Disadvantages: Loss of control/ownership, teamwork challenges, miscommunication, different working styles

Fun Facts & Real-World 💡

  • Charles Babbage's analytic engine was the ancestor of modern computers!
  • Steve Jobs was a product champion, not always the original inventor.
  • Most products today are created by multidisciplinary teams, not lone inventors.
  • Diverse teams help avoid groupthink and spark creativity.
