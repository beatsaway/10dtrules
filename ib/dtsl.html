🌍 What is the IB Diploma Programme?

The IB DP is a rigorous, balanced education for ages 16–19, preparing students for university and life. It encourages curiosity, compassion, intercultural understanding, and open-mindedness. Students develop skills in thinking, research, social, self-management, and communication.

🧭 Course Description & Aims

The DP design technology course develops internationally minded people who understand design and technology's role in creating a better world. Inquiry and problem-solving are central, using the design cycle to structure analysis, solution development, and evaluation. Students gain critical-thinking and design skills, applying knowledge ethically and practically.

  • Curiosity and independent learning about the technological world
  • Exploring concepts with local and global significance
  • Critical and creative thinking to solve complex problems
  • Confident, creative communication and collaboration
  • Integrity, honesty, and responsibility in design
  • Appreciation of global cultures and perspectives
  • Willingness to explore new ideas and defend proposals
  • Balance and well-being through design and technology
  • Empathy and respect for others and the environment
  • Reflecting on the impact of design and technology on society and the environment

🤝 The Group 4 Project

A collaborative activity where students from different sciences work together, sharing concepts and appreciating the environmental, social, and ethical implications of science and technology. Emphasis is on interdisciplinary cooperation and scientific processes.

🏆 Sample SL Questions

Sample (Paper 1): Which phrase best reflects the philosophy of the circular economy?
A. Cradle to cradle
B. Cradle to grave
C. Made to be made again
D. Take, make, dispose
Model Answer: A. Cradle to cradle. This phrase reflects the circular economy's goal of designing products so that materials can be reused or recycled continuously, minimizing waste.
Sample (Paper 2): Explain how the use of "design for the environment" software assists designers in choosing materials.
Model Answer: Such software helps designers compare the environmental impacts of different materials, such as energy use, recyclability, and pollution, so they can select options that are more sustainable and eco-friendly.
Sample (Paper 2): Discuss why the use of thermoplastic renders a product green but not sustainable.
Model Answer: Thermoplastics can be recycled, making them a 'green' choice. However, if they are made from non-renewable resources or not recycled properly, they are not truly sustainable in the long term.
