Concept Modeling 💡🧠

Why does it matter? Concept models help us think, communicate, and solve problems—before we ever build a thing! 🛠️✨

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Conceptual model 💡 – An idea or mental model to help understand and solve problems
  • Ideation 🤔 – Generating lots of ideas for solutions
  • Iteration 🔄 – Repeating and improving ideas over time
  • Prototype 🛠️ – A physical or virtual model built to test ideas
  • Communication 🗣️ – Sharing ideas with others (team, client, manufacturer)

What is Conceptual Modeling? 💡

Definition

Conceptual models are ideas that exist in the mind. Designers use them to mentally simulate solutions, consider how products/systems/services should behave, and imagine how users will understand them.

The Process

Start with lots of ideas (ideation), narrow them down, combine or improve them, and repeat (iteration) until you have the best solution. Do as much as possible in your mind before building anything!

Why Concept Models Matter 🌟

Save Time & Money

Mental models are cheaper and faster than building lots of prototypes. You can test ideas in your head before making anything physical.

Communication

Concept models help you explain your thinking to teammates, clients, and manufacturers. If you can't communicate your idea, it stays stuck in your head!

Visualization

Helps turn abstract ideas into something you (and others) can see and understand—using graphic, physical, or virtual models.

How to Communicate Concept Models 🗣️

Flowcharts & Diagrams

Show how things work or how ideas connect.
Example: A flowchart for a user login process.

Physical Models

Clay models, mock-ups, or non-functioning prototypes.
Example: A clay car model for a new BMW concept.

Napkin Sketches

Quick, rough drawings to get ideas out of your head and onto paper.
Example: Sketching yacht ideas on a napkin.

Virtual Models

3D models made in software to show clients before building anything physical.
Example: A 3D-printed boat part designed in CAD.

Advantages & Disadvantages 👍👎

Advantages

  • Helps with project planning
  • Makes abstract ideas visual and understandable
  • Promotes communication and feedback
  • Cheaper and faster than building every idea

Disadvantages

  • May lack detail or be too simple
  • Scale or materials may not match reality
  • Can be misunderstood or miscommunicated
  • Some people struggle to visualize from models

Fun Facts & Real-World Examples 💡

  • Car companies often make full-size clay models before building a real prototype!
  • Some of the best ideas start as napkin sketches—think of the first iPhone concept!
  • Virtual models can be shared instantly with clients anywhere in the world.
