Product Standardization & Types 📏🌍

How do standards, types, and pricing shape the market? 🛠️

Key Vocabulary 📚

  • Standardization – Making products/components uniform across markets
  • Component Standardization – Interchangeable parts (e.g., USB plugs, screws)
  • Industry-wide Standards – Agreed norms across an industry (e.g., shipping containers)
  • Trigger Product – Product bought to satisfy a need (e.g., smartphone)
  • Incremental Product – Add-on product (e.g., phone case)
  • Brick & Mortar – Physical store
  • Online – E-commerce store
  • Hybrid – Mix of online and physical shopping
  • Cost-plus Pricing – Add profit margin to cost
  • Demand Pricing – Set price based on demand
  • Competitor Pricing – Set price based on rivals
  • Psychological Pricing – Price to seem cheaper (e.g., $9.99)
  • Promotion – Communicating product info to customers

Core Concepts 🧠

Why Standardization Matters

  • Enables global trade and compatibility
  • Reduces confusion and increases trust
  • Examples: metric system, CE mark, USB plugs

Component & Industry-wide Standards

  • Component: interchangeable parts (e.g., screws, plugs)
  • Industry-wide: e.g., shipping containers, bolt sizes

Types of Products

  • Trigger product: bought to satisfy a need (e.g., phone)
  • Incremental product: add-on (e.g., phone case)

Place: Where Products Are Sold

  • Brick & mortar: physical store
  • Online: e-commerce
  • Hybrid: order online, pick up in store

Price: How Products Are Priced

  • Cost-plus: add profit margin
  • Demand: set by demand
  • Competitor: set by rivals
  • Psychological: e.g., $9.99

Promotion: Communicating Value

  • Advertising, publicity, personal selling
  • Informs and persuades customers

Fun Fact 🌍

  • There are only three countries in the world that don't use the metric system: the US, Liberia, and Myanmar!
  • Shipping containers revolutionized global trade by standardizing size and handling.
